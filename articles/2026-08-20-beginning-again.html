The Practice of Beginning Again

August 20, 2026 · Spirituality

Every morning the sun rises whether we notice it or not. The world does not ask our permission. It simply begins again, and again, and again. This is the first and most honest spiritual lesson available to any of us: that starting over is not a failure but the nature of things.

We tend to carry yesterday into today like luggage we refuse to set down. The conversation that went wrong. The mistake at work. The thing someone said three years ago that still stings when we think about it long enough. We drag these moments behind us and wonder why we feel so tired.

But the soul does not actually store pain the way the mind does. The soul is more like a river. It moves through whatever landscape it encounters. It does not hold grudges against the rocks. It does not resent the drought or fear the flood. It simply flows, and in flowing, it remains itself.

There is a difference between remembering and carrying. Remembering is natural. It is how we learn. Carrying is something else entirely. Carrying is when we take a memory and make it part of our identity. I am the person this happened to. We say it quietly, almost unconsciously, and then we live inside that sentence as though it were a room we could not leave.

Spiritual practice across every tradition points toward the same stubborn truth. You are not your worst moment. You are not your best moment either. You are the awareness that holds all moments and remains untouched by them. This is what the mystics mean when they talk about the self that precedes the self. There is something in you that was here before the story began, and it will be here after the story ends.

The practice is not complicated. It is just difficult. Notice when you are carrying something. Feel the weight of it in your body. The tightness in your chest. The tension in your jaw. The slight holding in your breath. That is what it feels like to refuse the present moment in favor of a past one.

Then set it down. Not forever. Just for now. Just long enough to breathe once without the weight. That single breath is the entire path. Everything else is commentary.

People ask how often they should meditate, which tradition they should follow. But the deeper answer is that spiritual life is not about accumulation. It is about release. We do not need more beliefs. We need fewer walls between ourselves and the raw experience of being alive.

The teacher Ram Dass spent decades studying practices before distilling them into a phrase that changed how millions understood their own lives. His classic work Be Here Now remains one of the most accessible entry points into this understanding. It is not a book you read once. It reads you differently each time you return to it.

Because that is the thing about beginning again. You do not arrive at a destination. You simply become more fluent in the language of starting over. More willing to set down what you were carrying. More honest about how much of it you ever needed.

The sun rises tomorrow whether we notice or not. The invitation is to notice. To be present for the beginning. To let it be enough.

Today is a beginning. So was yesterday. So is every breath from here forward. The practice is not to become someone new. It is to remember who you were before you forgot.
